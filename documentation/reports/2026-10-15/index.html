Proof of concept#

Interactive panels built from the round-2 log: the new phase windows, and every configuration's WIS by phase and horizon against the baseline of the same seed.

Pick a phase and up to four configurations. The chart shows how much each one changes WIS relative to the baseline trained with the same seed; the band is the spread over the 3 seeds. In the peak phase every peak-emphasis arm sits below the baseline at 1-2 weeks and above it at 4 weeks.

The old scheme assigned whole months; the new one assigns by date because April is split. Peak moved from Dec-Jan to Jan-Feb, so round-1 and round-2 peak numbers are not comparable and the reports refuse to compare them.

October

The windows leave October unassigned. It is treated as off-season until confirmed; changing it is one constant, and saved forecasts can be re-scored without retraining.

Results in brief#

XGBoost, 2025-26 season, 9 forecast dates, US excluded, peak = Jan 1 - end of Feb, 3 seeds.

  • Pooled. Baseline peak WIS 107.3 with a seed sd of 1.2. The best arm, peak weight 2x, is 1.9 lower, and lower in every seed, but that is under 2%. Approaching-peak weights are slightly worse at every value; the 52-week window is much worse (+68).
  • By horizon. Peak weight and peak oversampling cut 1- and 2-week peak WIS by 4-13 and raise 4-week peak WIS by 9-22, with every seed agreeing on the direction. The 3-week horizon is roughly unchanged.
  • Oversampling vs weighting. They behave almost the same, as expected for XGBoost: k copies of a row act like weight k unless rows are subsampled.
  • Overall WIS stays within 1 of baseline for every arm except the window.

The pooled score hid a horizon trade-off. The per-horizon models make it actionable: keep the baseline for 3-4 weeks and use peak emphasis for 1-2 weeks.

Caveats

One evaluation season; each peak cell rests on 2-3 forecast dates. The "short horizons only" combination was spotted on this season, so it is a hypothesis until it is tested on a season it was not chosen on.

Draft paper outline#

Working title: An Agentic Test Bench for Evaluating and Refining Epidemic Forecasting Models. The outline marks what exists, what is in progress and what is planned; the full draft is documentation/paper/outline.md.

Model bank and selection

exists

One model contract, 9 families, in-house models plug in by dotted path; rolling-origin comparison on the pinned split, incumbent chosen by metric and phase.

Constrained improvement loop

exists

Diagnose, propose one validated action, retrain, keep or revert; every step logged. The LLM edits configuration, never model code.

Knowledge bank

exists (v1)

Lab rules as curated entries, experiment outcomes as experiential entries, cited at every proposal; can be switched off for an ablation.

Phase x horizon evaluation

exists

WIS by phase window and horizon, multi-seed, with re-scoring of saved forecasts under any phase scheme.

Vanilla vs refined results

planned

Needs the in-house model and an agreed protocol: refine on one season, score on another.

Comparison against non-agent search

planned

The same retrain budget spent by random or Optuna search, and by a rule-based policy that applies the bank's recommendations, to show what the agent adds.

Open questions#

Is October off-season or growth?

Currently off-season. Growth would make the window Oct 1 - Dec 31. Saved forecasts can be re-scored either way.

Which in-house model first, and who can grant code access?

This is the agreed success metric and the main blocker for the paper's results section.

Venue and deadline?

The plan assumes a January venue with a February fallback; the page limit decides how much of the system description survives.

How should vanilla vs refined be measured?

Proposal: let the agent refine on 2024-25 forecast dates, then score the vanilla and refined models on the 2025-26 dates, so no model is scored on the season it was tuned on.

What should the agent be compared against?

Proposal: the same number of retrains spent by random or Optuna search over the same actions, and by a rule-based policy that applies the knowledge bank directly.

How many models should the paper report?

The 9 repo families include untuned Nixtla examples; the framing ("we tried N models") depends on how many lab models can be onboarded before the deadline.

export KMP_DUPLICATE_LIB_OK=TRUE OMP_NUM_THREADS=1

# round 2: one process per seed, then merge

  python scripts/experiments/peak_rectification.py --seeds $s --windows 52 --out outputs/experiments/pr2-seed$s &
